Smart dispatch: no-show warnings, empty-leg chaining, importer memory

Three things the data already knew and nobody was told.

1. No-show risk on the booking card (company + admin, never the driver).
   lib/bookingRisk.ts reads what is already in the database - the 1h
   confirmation reminder that went unanswered, a pickup time that has passed
   with the trip never started, a previous job ending twenty minutes before
   this one, a phone that stopped reporting a position - and turns it into one
   sentence early enough to pick up the phone. Read-only: it changes no
   booking, sends no push, reassigns nobody.

   Two rules keep it worth believing. A driver's record never raises an alarm
   on its own; it is capped so it can at most double a concern a live signal
   already raised. And a signal we cannot read is not a bad signal - a driver
   who never shared a location is silent, not suspicious.

2. Empty-leg chaining in dispatch. A driver who drops a group in Kazbegi at
   one and would drive home empty is the cheapest possible driver for a
   three o'clock job out of Kazbegi, and dispatch had no idea he was there.
   drivers_finishing_near finds him without showing anyone another company's
   booking: it answers with a subset of the driver ids the caller already
   holds, for a place the caller itself named.

   Implemented as a sort tier beside coverageTier, so it only ever reorders.
   Wave 1 still fills to the same size, wave 2 still carries everyone else,
   the newcomer seats still hold, and with nothing marked the order is exactly
   what it was. Coverage still outranks the calendar: a driver who told us he
   does not work a region has said something about himself his schedule has
   not.

3. The importer remembers each company's wording. The model is asked for the
   document's own heading per field; any heading the built-in label table does
   not know is remembered against that company and merged into the next parse,
   so the second file from an operator is usually read for free and exactly.
   Built-in labels win every collision, lessons are per company, and a heading
   the model marked uncertain is never written down - so learning cannot turn
   a document that parses today into one that does not.

Verified: 31 scoring cases and 10 wave cases pass, including "nobody is ever
dropped from a wave" and "with no marks the order is pure score"; tsc clean;
no new lint warnings; the new SQL exercised against live rows.
